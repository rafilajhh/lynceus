# Regulation and standard notes (summary of cited sources, not legal text)

These notes summarise the sources already cited in the Lynceus research files (docs/research/) and in the vendored decision engine (lynceus_engine). They are working notes written for the demo. They are not the text of any regulation or standard, and they are not legal advice. Read the cited source before relying on any figure.

## REG-HH1 — 40 CFR Part 98 Subpart HH, Equation HH-1 and Table HH-1
The gas generation model of the Lynceus engine follows Equation HH-1 of 40 CFR 98.343(a)(1), the EPA methane generation equation for municipal solid waste landfills: G_CH4 is the sum over waste placed in year x of W_x times MCF times DOC times DOC_F times F times 16/12 times (e^(-k(T-x-1)) - e^(-k(T-x))). The constants come from Table HH-1 to Subpart HH (revision at 89 FR 31940, effective 2025-01-01). Table HH-1 gives MCF = 1.0, DOC_F = 0.5 and F = 0.5, where F is a default that a measurement can replace. For waste composition it gives the degradable organic carbon (DOC) of each material: food 0.15, garden 0.20, paper 0.40, wood 0.43, textile 0.24, diaper 0.24, sludge 0.05, inert 0.00. It also gives a range of the decay rate k for each material. Footnote c of the table says to use the lesser k value when potential evapotranspiration exceeds precipitation plus recirculated leachate, and the greater value when it does not, so a wetter material decays faster. The engine applies the equation to one batch of known mass and known age, which is a modelling choice of Lynceus and not a regulatory calculation. HH-1 is an anaerobic first-order decay model, so it does not strictly apply to a fresh aerobic load.

## REG-AP42 — EPA AP-42 Chapter 2.4, landfill gas
EPA AP-42 Chapter 2.4 (final, August 2024) describes landfill gas. At steady state the gas is about 55% methane, 40% carbon dioxide and 5% nitrogen (AP-42 page 2.4-3), which Lynceus treats as a ceiling for methane and not a target. Section 2.4.4 describes four decomposition phases: phase I aerobic, phase II transition as oxygen is used up, phase III the onset of methane formation, and phase IV steady state. AP-42 describes the phases in words and states that their durations vary with landfill conditions, so the hour limits in Lynceus are a model assumption. Table 2.4-1 gives default trace-gas concentrations, for example hydrogen sulfide at 36 ppmv with data quality rating B. Table 2.4-2 gives further species such as benzene, toluene and non-methane organic compounds as hexane, with separate figures for co-disposal. These values are measurements of aged, anaerobic landfill gas. Using them for a fresh load is an extrapolation, and the true strength of a fresh bin is not cited. Source: EPA LMOP, https://www.epa.gov/lmop/basic-information-about-landfill-gas, and the AP-42 Chapter 2.4 tables.

## REG-EN15359 — EN 15359 and ISO 21640 solid recovered fuel classes
EN 15359:2011 gives the specification of solid recovered fuel: particle shape and size, moisture, ash, net calorific value (NCV), chlorine and heavy metals (https://www.zkg.de/en/artikel/zkg_Solid_Recovered_Fuels_Specifications_and_classes-2067874.html). ISO 21640:2021, Solid recovered fuels, Specifications and classes, classifies a fuel by NCV, chlorine and mercury (https://www.iso.org/standard/71309.html). ISO 21654:2021 covers the determination of calorific value (https://www.iso.org/standard/71319.html). A common cement kiln specification quoted in the research is NCV above 14 MJ/kg, moisture below 15%, chlorine below 0.8% and ash below 15% (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13049024). The class boundaries of the standards are not cited in this repository, so Lynceus does not assign a class. NCV, ash and chlorine as a share of dry mass need a laboratory analysis of the material. The gas phase cannot give the mass of the fuel, so the engine reports these three parameters as needing analysis and never estimates them.

## REG-NIOSH-H2S — NIOSH exposure limit for hydrogen sulfide
The Lynceus engine checks the peak sampled hydrogen sulfide (H2S) concentration against the NIOSH recommended exposure limit (REL) for H2S, a ceiling of 10 ppm over 10 minutes (NIOSH Pocket Guide, hydrogen sulfide). A peak above 10 ppmv counts as an exceedance, and the ratio of peak to limit is reported. A gas without a published limit in the engine is reported as unchecked, and no limit is inferred from a similar compound. For comparison, the AP-42 Table 2.4-1 default for H2S in aged landfill gas is 36 ppmv, which is a landfill measurement and not a reading from a truck. The engine also holds NIOSH RELs, as 8-hour time weighted averages, for carbon monoxide (35 ppm), benzene (0.1 ppm), toluene (100 ppm), vinyl chloride (1 ppm) and methyl mercaptan (0.5 ppm), and uses the n-hexane REL (50 ppm) as the proxy for VOC.

## REG-IDN-RDF — Indonesian gate and road-load rules
The Indonesian rules in the research files are about the receiving gate and the road load of the truck. The PUPR TPA receiving SOP (Rakernis Adipura 2019, Kementerian PUPR Ditjen Cipta Karya) has the gate officer record six fields: plate number, driver, entry time, waste source, weight or volume by weighbridge, and a check of the waste condition, in which hazardous (B3), medical and industrial waste is refused (https://bulelengkab.go.id/informasi/download/pengelolaan-tpa-yang-baik-landfill-52.pdf). For road load, UU No. 22/2009 (LLAJ) Article 169 bars loads above the set dimension or weight, with a fine of up to Rp 500.000. PP No. 55/2012 sets the standard maximum axle load (MST) at 8 t per axle, and 10 t on class I national roads. Permenhub PM 134/2015 covers the road weighbridges (UPPKB), and PP No. 30/2021 covers the supervision of overloading and its sanctions (https://isuzuniaga.com/regulasi-muatan-sumbu-terberat-mst-dan-dampaknya-bagi-truk). The research files give no Indonesian regulation that sets a quality specification for RDF.
